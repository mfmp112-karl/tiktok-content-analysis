The frameworks behind this

The playbook my checks are built on, credited to @teezytheturtle. I am setting it out so you can use it without me.

1. Find the audience by studying who already has it

Somebody is already making content for the people you want. Read how they name their audience in their bio, what they post about, what register they write in. That is your positioning research, and it is free.

2. The profile converts, the content only delivers

Reach gets someone to a video; the profile turns them into a follower. Four things do that work: a picture that still reads at comment size, a bio of three or four scannable lines that names who it is for and what they get, a link so the attention has somewhere to go, and pinned posts that show your best work first. The audit page has the specifics.

3. A unique style is found by subtraction

Test several kinds of content, drop the ones that do not travel, replace them, repeat. Your feed currently reads as about 7.3 distinct styles. That loop is what the theme table on this report is measuring — Keep, Ditch and Test more are the three moves in it. The trap is quitting a style before you have made enough of it to know.

4. The hook is the whole first second

A strong opening buys you the rest of the video, and a script that holds after the hook is what turns a view into watch time. Vary the shape of your openings — repeating the same opener is one of the least visible ways to lose reach, and the hooks page checks for exactly that.

5. Post often enough to learn

Daily is the advice, and the reason is not the algorithm — it is that you cannot find your style from six posts. Frequency is what makes everything else in this report measurable. The timing page tests whether it has shown up in your own numbers yet.

Method, limits and credits

How I got these numbers

I read the catalogue via invented data (see examples/make_sample.py), which returns the same public metrics any visitor can see: views, likes, comments, shares, caption, duration and upload time. Nothing was downloaded and no engagement was automated.

I grouped the themes by tf-idf + k-means (pure python). Where several counts fitted about equally well I took the simpler split, because a feed shattered into twelve themes is not something anyone can act on.

I test every comparison with Welch's t-test on log-transformed views. View counts are wildly skewed, so without the log one viral post carries a whole bucket and I end up telling you something that is not true. I never call a group of fewer than eight posts significant, however large the gap looks — “solid evidence” means p < 0.01 and “early signal” means p < 0.05. Everything else I report as not settled.

I converted upload times in this machine's local timezone, so “best hour” is in your clock, not UTC.

What I genuinely cannot see

Watch time, retention curves, traffic sources, follower growth over time and who your audience is are things TikTok shows only to the account owner, inside their own analytics. I cannot reach them for an account I do not own, and neither can anything else. A good deal of creator advice rests on those numbers; where it does, I have tested what I can measure instead and told you which is which rather than inventing a stand-in.
